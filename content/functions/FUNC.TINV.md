# TINV

## What it does

`TINV(probability,deg_freedom)` returns a nonnegative Student-t critical magnitude for a **two-tailed** probability. It is the compatibility name for the two-tail inverse.

## Try it

`=TINV(0.5,1)` returns `1` under the current explicit Cauchy control. Half the one-degree distribution lies beyond plus or minus one.

## Quirks

Degrees are truncated. Invalid degrees or probabilities give `#NUM!`; nonnumeric inputs give `#VALUE!`. The public page admits probability one in its range wording, while the current shared inverse requires `0<p<1`. For a one-tail upper critical value use twice that tail probability in the two-tail inverse, within its admitted range.

## How to implement

For degree one, the retained route is `1/TAN(PI()*p/2)`, with a separate exact `p=0.5` return of one. For higher degrees the code currently brackets and inverts the decreasing two-tail beta-ratio probability. Public-composition readings support a `sqrt(v*(1/BETA.INV(p,v/2,0.5)-1))` construction on their scope, but the current beta inverse still differs; the code therefore retains its generic inverse. Do not describe that unresolved composition as a shipped exact route.

### FN-TINV-001: TINV for p up to 0.85 is SQRT(v*(1/z - 1)) on a binary64 beta coordinate z, so its last digits follow z

*Detail for matching Excel's bits; measured; recorded 2026-11-11.* Applies to T.INV.2T, TINV.

For df >= 2 and p <= 0.85, the recorded TINV answers lie in the image of the map t = SQRT(v *
(1/z - 1)) over binary64 values z, with v the degrees of freedom and z the inverse of the
regularized beta at (v/2, 1/2). Because z is a double, the t found this way can sit many units
from the true root of the t distribution: TINV(0.85, 25) is 176 units in the last place from
the root's t only through this map. DNA Calc takes, of the two adjacent doubles that bracket
the beta root, the one whose forward value is nearer p. On the full bank this took TINV from 15
to 22 matched of 32 rows (+8 and -1: TINV(0.003, 25) was lost) and its worst miss from 173 to
17 units; the oracle corpus passed count went from 76,765 to 76,769 (CONFIDENCE.T, T.INV.2T and
TINV rows fixed). For p near 1 Excel uses another route: at p = 0.97 three rows are still 10 to
17 units off the t-space bisection DNA Calc keeps there.
