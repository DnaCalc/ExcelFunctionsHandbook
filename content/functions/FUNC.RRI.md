# RRI

## What it does

`RRI(nper,pv,fv)` returns the equivalent compound rate per period between a starting and final amount, without intermediate cash flows. Mathematically it is `(fv/pv)^(1/nper)-1`.

## Try it

`=RRI(2,64,100)` returns about `0.25`: the growth factor is 1.5625, whose square root is 1.25. Two periods compound that rate from 64 to 100.

## Quirks

The recorded current kernel rejects nonpositive or subnormal period counts before its equality shortcut. It normalises subnormal value inputs to zero, checks equal values before sign guards, and admits two negative amounts with a positive ratio. A zero present value or opposite nonzero signs gives `#NUM!` under those rules.

## How to implement

Use stored x87 division for `base=fv/pv` and for `reciprocal=1/nper`. A normalised zero base returns minus one. For exactly one period, retain the quotient directly as the powered value; otherwise use the raw measured LN/product/EXP power chain, not the worksheet POWER square-root shortcut. Store the final subtraction of one. The current helper also applies finite-result guards. This preserves cancellation-sensitive stores near equal amounts; native pow followed by subtraction can differ substantially on small rates.
