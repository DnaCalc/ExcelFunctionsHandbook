# FORECAST.ETS

## What it does

`FORECAST.ETS` predicts a future value from a timed series, using the AAA version of exponential smoothing.

`FORECAST.ETS(target_date, values, timeline, [seasonality], [data_completion], [aggregation])`

- **`target_date`** is the point to predict, a date or number after the end of the history.
- **`values`** is the history.
- **`timeline`** is the matching dates or numbers, with a constant nonzero step. It need not be sorted.
- **`seasonality`** is 1 to detect the pattern (default), 0 for none, or a positive pattern length up to 8,760.
- **`data_completion`** is 1 to fill missing points with the average of their neighbours (default), or 0 to treat
  them as zeros. Up to 30% of points may be missing.
- **`aggregation`** combines values sharing a timestamp: 0 AVERAGE (default), then SUM, COUNT, COUNTA, MIN, MAX and MEDIAN.

## Quirks

- `#NUM!`: the target precedes the end of the history, no constant step exists, or `seasonality` is invalid or above 8,760.
- `#N/A`: `values` and `timeline` differ in size.
- `#VALUE!`: Microsoft's timeline section lists this for duplicate timeline values, while its aggregation
  section says duplicates are combined. The two statements conflict, and no reading is recorded here to settle it.

## How to implement

No implementation exists yet. It needs the AAA exponential smoothing fit, the detection of a constant timeline step, seasonality
detection, the data-completion and aggregation rules, and recorded Excel answers to fix the optimiser's stopping
rules. Forecast values depend on those rules, so they must be measured and not assumed.

## Version differences

Microsoft says these functions are not available in Excel for the web, iOS or Android. No Excel answers for this function are recorded here, so no comparison between Excel builds exists.

## Alternatives

- `FORECAST.LINEAR` for a straight-line forecast.
- `TREND` and `GROWTH` for fitted series.
- The Forecast Sheet command, which builds on the same family.

### FN-FORECAST.ETS-001: FORECAST.ETS fits histories of four or fewer points nonseasonally and extends those under seven by regression

*Detail for matching Excel's bits; inferred; recorded 2026-11-11.*

In DNA Calc's implementation of FORECAST.ETS, every prepared history of four or fewer points is
fitted as a nonseasonal series, whether the seasonality argument is automatic (1), an explicit
2, or 0. For a history shorter than seven points the starting series is extended to seven
points before the search: the trend is the least-squares slope of the points in order (computed
in plain binary64), and the point at index j (from the history length up to 6) is y[j mod 2] +
(j - j mod 2) * trend. DNA Calc computed these histories under this rule and matched Excel on
199 of the 204 recorded short-history cells; the other five differ from Excel by 1 to 3 units
in the last place. One recorded Excel forecast, FORECAST.ETS(5, {2,7,-1,4}, {1,2,3,4}, 2) =
1.5926883521785709, is held as a unit test. Before this change the same histories were refused
unless the slope and every product and sum were exact in binary64; on the oracle corpus the
passed count went from 76,480 to 76,759 and the refused count from 956 to 672, with no
previously matched row lost.

### FN-FORECAST.ETS-002: Seasonal FORECAST.ETS centers 2p sliding moving-average initials, starts smoothing at j=0, and linearly interpolates fractional CONFINT variance

*Detail for matching Excel's bits; inferred; recorded 2026-11-22.* Applies to FORECAST.ETS, FORECAST.ETS.CONFINT, FORECAST.ETS.SEASONALITY, FORECAST.ETS.STAT.

In Excel 16.0.20527, seasonal AAA FORECAST.ETS builds initial state over m = min(len, 5 * p)
observations (extending histories shorter than ceil(3.5 * p) by tau = sum_q
regression_slope(phase_q) / (p * p) via raw-moment slopes). It computes a subtract-first 2*p
sliding moving average sum = (sum - e[t - 1]) + e[t + 2*p - 1], T[t] = sum / (2*p), averages
detrended e[t + p/2 - 1] - T[t] per phase to form raw[q], centers seasons[q] = raw[q] - center
with pre-centered initial level = (y[0] - raw[0]) + center, and sets initial trend T_0 =
regression_slope(e[j] - seasons[j % p]). The smoothing loop starts at t = -1 (level =
initial.level - initial.trend) and runs from j = 0, so err[0] enters MAE, RMSE, SSE, and state
updates. FORECAST.ETS.STAT types 1-3 show Excel stores 6-digit rounded binary64 smoothing
constants (such as 0.001, 0.0833333, 0.166667); seasonal_general re-centers seasons and adds
them via x87 PC64; and FORECAST.ETS.CONFINT linearly interpolates mse * horizon_variance at
fractional horizons (from 0 on h in (0, 1)) before sqrt and x87 multiplication by z.
