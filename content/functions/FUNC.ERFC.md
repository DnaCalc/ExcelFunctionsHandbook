# ERFC

## What it does

`ERFC(x)` is the normalised integral of `exp(-t*t)` from x to infinity. Mathematically it is `1-erf(x)`, but a direct tail calculation retains information when erf is close to one.

## Try it

`=ERFC(0.25)` is about `0.723673609831763`; `=ERFC(0)` is `1`. A large positive argument has a small positive tail, whereas a large negative argument approaches two.

## Quirks

Nonnumeric input gives `#VALUE!`. Subtracting a value rounded to one from one can erase a representable tail. The PRECISE spelling is not a whole-domain accuracy guarantee. The current kernel flushes a final subnormal tail to zero.

## How to implement

For `0<x<0.5`, the current route subtracts the [stored ERF rational](FUNC.ERF.md#how-to-implement) from one using binary64 subtraction. For `x>0.5`, the identified outer stages are `z=R(x*x)`, `E=EXP(-z)` stored, and `result=R(E*F(x))`. `R` is the stored 64-bit-significand operation. Excel’s own rational factor `F` is **still open**; the code uses Cody coefficients as a stand-in, so copying that table is not a solved Excel tail recipe.

For negative arguments below `-0.5`, the current reconstruction uses `R(2-positive_tail(abs(x)))`. The exact half point and tiny nonpositive route retain their separate boundary body. Worked below-half case: the reconstructed ERF at `0.25` is `0.276326390168237`; subtracting from one gives approximately `0.723673609831763`. For the upper stage at `x=1`, `z=1` and `E` is about `0.36787944117144233`; the remaining factor must still be supplied. This identifies what can be implemented faithfully from the evidence and what remains a numerical substitute.

## Alternatives

[ERF](FUNC.ERF.md) computes the central integral; [NORM.S.DIST](FUNC.NORM.S.DIST.md) expresses normal probabilities. Use a scaled complementary error function internally to avoid overflow when designing a mathematical tail algorithm.

### FN-ERF.PRECISE-001: Below 0.5, ERF is a stored (4, 5) rational from a table not yet identified

*Detail for matching Excel's bits; measured; recorded 2026-11-04.* Applies to ERF, ERF.PRECISE, ERFC, ERFC.PRECISE, GAUSS.

With RN(v) meaning v rounded to 64 bits on the x87 unit and then stored to binary64,
ERF.PRECISE for |x| < 0.5 is x*N(t)/D(t) with t = RN(x*x), p = RN(x*N), y = RN(p/D), and N and
D Horner sums in t in which every product and every sum is RN. N has degree 4 and D degree 5,
and the coefficients read off Excel's answers, order 0 upward, are N: 0x3ff1df5e70c7956b,
0x3fc201d8627a7d2c, 0x3fa6f289d165b303, 0x3f5d93a5813e372c, 0x3f2932767df264b2; D:
0x3fefada15778ece6, 0x3fdd191aca8d2499, 0x3fb79f85ba68803e, 0x3f856de5da86af12,
0x3f4609751b5b6488, 0x3ef4f20a473a5ab0. The rational stops at 0.5 itself. It equals erf to a
flat 9E-17 up to 0.5, and its error climbs at once beyond, so 0.5 is its design interval and
not Cody's 0.46875. No published table has been matched: Cody's 1969 rational is refuted by its
linear terms, and Cephes has the same shape with different ratios. Evidence record
EV-DIST-0026, which predates this note, attributes ERF.PRECISE and ERFC.PRECISE to the a < 1
branches of the NSWC GRATIO routine and says no coefficient table exists; below 0.5 this note
replaces that attribution. Because every step rounds, answers need not be correctly rounded: of
the 654 recorded rows of build 20131 in [0.25, 0.5), 400 differ from the correctly rounded erf,
by up to 3 units, and small arguments can be 1 unit high, as below. ERFC and ERFC.PRECISE below
0.5 are 1 less this value, and GAUSS at tiny arguments takes half of it at its stored
z/sqrt(2).

### FN-ERFC-001: Above 0.5, ERFC rounds x*x before the exponential, so the tail drifts by hundreds of units

*Excel differs from the mathematical answer; measured; recorded 2026-11-04.* Applies to ERFC, ERFC.PRECISE.

With RN(v) meaning v rounded to 64 bits on the x87 unit and then stored to binary64, ERFC for x
>= 0.5 behaves as Q = E*F with z = RN(x*x) and E = exp(-z) stored to binary64, then multiplied
by a factor F that is not identified. So the rounding error of x*x reaches the answer at full
size. At x = 26.543000000000003, x*x exceeds z by 4.64E-14, which at the size of this answer is
about 212 units, and Excel's answer is 212 units above the correctly rounded erfc. Near x =
5.93 the same effect is 23 units. For small x, where x*x is exact or nearly so, the answer
stays within a few units.

Basis (measured): 220,000 Excel answers from 0.5 to 26.5 (build 20527) compared with E times
the true exp(x*x)*erfc(x). With z = RN(x*x) and E stored, the residual stays within about 10
units of 2^-53 at every x. At x = 24 the exact exp(-x*x) spreads with a standard deviation of
287 units, Cody's and fdlibm's fractional splits of x*x spread the same, and plain binary64 x*x
leaves outliers of up to 1,024 units.

Inferred, not measured: F is not a closed form: y = RN(E*RN(F)) with any offset matches 40% of
answers at best. Its noise tracks about a dozen smooth curves, consistent with two polynomials
of degree about 8 in x with shapes near Cephes' erfc P and Q, but that is a reading of shape
only, and no table has been matched.

- Open: F, so the last bits: with a stand-in for F, recorded rows match ERFC 234 of 544 and ERFC.PRECISE 1,062 of 1,863, with no answer more than 6 units away.
- Open: The mean of the residual is flat with sharp steps of about 0.4 units, at x = 1.11567925... and 1.1217 among about 80 in [0.5, 27]. No stored polynomial value varies that slowly, and this is unexplained.
