# ODDFPRICE

## What it does

`ODDFPRICE` computes clean price per 100 face value for a security with an irregular first coupon. Its dates are settlement, maturity, issue and first_coupon. They are followed by coupon rate, yield, redemption, frequency and optional basis.

## Try it

`=ODDFPRICE(DATE(2027,2,1),DATE(2027,10,1),DATE(2027,1,1),DATE(2027,4,1),0.06,0,100,2,0)` gives a price near `104`. The first coupon spans January to April, a short half-year coupon. Under 30/360 its payment is 1.5; the regular maturity coupon is three; accrued interest at February settlement is 0.5. At zero yield the clean price is `100+1.5+3-0.5=104`.

## Quirks

Basis defaults to zero: 0 US 30/360, 1 actual/actual, 2 actual/360, 3 actual/365, 4 European 30/360. Dates and selectors are truncated under their documented rules. Use DATE values rather than locale-dependent date text. The required date order is `issue < settlement < first_coupon < maturity`. Frequency is one, two or four. Negative coupon rate, invalid selectors and invalid amount/domain cases produce errors. These schedules cannot be reduced to PRICE by changing one date.

## How to implement

Generate quasi-coupon periods aligned to first_coupon and maturity. Prorate the odd first coupon across those periods, discount it and later coupons/redemption, and subtract accrued interest. Long first periods can contain multiple quasi-coupons. The worked zero-yield example isolates the coupon and accrual arithmetic from discount powers. Calendar seams, long/short-period branches and residual numerical differences remain open where the recorded reports say so. The family combines a credited Apache-licensed ExcelFinancialFunctions translation in its odd-first kernel and calendar helpers with project-owned kernels and changes guided by recorded Excel answers.

## Version differences

No same-input comparison between named Excel builds is recorded for this function here. The documented behaviour below is not a measurement of every Excel release.

## Alternatives

[ODDFYIELD](FUNC.ODDFYIELD.md) solves yield from the same first-coupon schedule. [ODDLPRICE](FUNC.ODDLPRICE.md) handles an irregular final coupon; [PRICE](FUNC.PRICE.md) handles regular coupons.

### FN-ODDFPRICE-001: ODDFPRICE splits short vs long odd-first periods, sums per-period fractions, and uses integer-exponent binexp

*Detail for matching Excel's bits; measured; recorded 2026-11-22.*

In Excel 16.0 builds 20026, 20131, and 20527, ODDFPRICE branches on whether DFC < E (short odd
first coupon) or DFC >= E (long odd first coupon spanning multiple quasi-coupon periods). In
the long branch it walks backward from first_coupon summing per-period day-count fractions (dci
/ nl) rather than using a single-period closed form, and for discount factors (1 +
yld/freq)^exp it uses binary exponentiation for non-negative integer exponents < 1024 and x87
pow otherwise, matching all 2,956 full-bank rows.
