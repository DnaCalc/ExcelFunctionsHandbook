# PV

## What it does

`PV(rate,nper,pmt,[fv],[type])` solves the present value of fixed payments and a final balance. Rate and period count must use the same interval. Type zero pays at the end of each period; type one at its beginning.

## Try it

`=PV(0,4,-250)` returns `1000`. Four negative payments are balanced by a positive present value. `=PV(0.25,1,0,1250)` is about `-1000`: a future inflow has a negative present outflow under this sign convention.

## Quirks

At least a payment or a future value must be supplied. Inflows and outflows have opposite signs. The current PV helper uses a small-rate band `abs(rate)<1e-12` for its linear branch; PMT’s separately measured zero-rate rule is exact equality. Do not transfer one function’s branch boundary to another.

## How to implement

For a mathematical route put `g=(1+r)^n` and `a=(1+r*type)*(g-1)/r`, then return `-(fv+pmt*a)/g`. At zero rate the expression has the limit `-(fv+pmt*n)`. The current helper implements that limit over its small-rate band, otherwise uses its power/annuity-term body and explicit nonfinite checks. Near zero, a log1p/expm1 formulation is numerically useful but changes intermediate rounding. Its last-bit behaviour is not solved by copying the PMT discount recipe.

## Alternatives

[NPV](FUNC.NPV.md) accepts varying equally spaced amounts; [XNPV](FUNC.XNPV.md) uses dates; [PMT](FUNC.PMT.md) and [FV](FUNC.FV.md) solve other annuity variables.
